  6. Widespread yet Unreliable: A Systematic Analysis of the Use of Presence Questionnaires
    Eugene Kukshinov, Joseph Tu, Kata Szita, Kaushall Senthil Nathan, and Lennart E. Nacke
    Interacting with Computers, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    Presence, as a psychological state, is typically assessed using questionnaires. While many researchers in this field assume that these self-report instruments are standardized, the reliability of such questionnaires remains uncertain. This knowledge gap challenges the accuracy and validity of data derived from studies assessing presence. Ensuring reliable and precise data collection and reporting is essential for the credibility of findings in presence research, because inaccuracies may cause errors in conclusions, which affects theoretical understandings, methodological approaches and practical applications. To address this issue, we conducted a systematic analysis of 397 empirical quantitative studies on presence. We investigated the use of presence scales, including applications, modifications, a variety of measures and reporting practices. We found that the majority of the presence studies modify questionnaires, do not re-validate them and improperly report their methods. Based on these findings, we propose solutions to enhance transparency and validation of the presence measurements.

  10. Walking into the Unknown: Manipulating Knowledge of the External Environment to Promote Presence in VR
    Eugene Kukshinov, Kaushall Senthil Nathan, Nicholas Bowman, Brendan Rooney, and Lennart E. Nacke
    International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    Presence is a key component of immersive virtual reality (VR) experiences, representing the psychological state of ’being there’ in the virtual environment. While various factors within the virtual simulation are known to influence presence, the role of a user’s awareness of their physical surroundings remains understudied. This study investigates how manipulating a user’s knowledge of the external environment before entering VR affects their sense of presence. In a randomized controlled experiment (N=23), we compared a condition where participants entered the VR environment normally to a condition where they entered with vision occluded (blindfolded) to restrict spatial awareness of the physical laboratory. We assessed presence using self-report questionnaires alongside behavioral measures, specifically tracking the velocity and intensity of reactions to virtual stimuli. Results indicate that vision occlusion significantly increased self-reported presence and led to more intense behavioral responses. These findings suggest that limiting external environmental awareness represents a cost-effective, easily implementable technique to enhance VR immersion.

  13. Beyond Competitive Gaming: How Casual Players Evaluate and Respond to Teammate Performance
    Kaushall Senthil Nathan, Jieun Lee, Derrick M. Wang, 3 more authors, and Lennart E. Nacke
    In Companion Proceedings of the Annual Symposium on Computer-Human Interaction in Play, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    Performance evaluation in multiplayer video games is heavily researched in competitive contexts like esports, where absolute metrics directly impact outcomes. However, it is unclear how these mechanisms operate in casual, cooperative game environments. This paper presents a controlled between-subjects experiment (N=23) using Overcooked 2 to investigate how casual players evaluate teammate performance. We manipulated confederate performance and collected NASA-TLX self-reports, observational behavioral data, and semi-structured interview responses. Our analysis reveals a significant discrepancy between behavioral responses and self-reported performance evaluation: while participants exhibited visible frustration in response to poor performance, their self-reports did not reflect this. Furthermore, we found that casual players evaluate performance using relative comparison rather than absolute criteria. Our results suggest that established competitive game evaluation models do not translate directly to casual settings, highlighting the need for comparative performance design in cooperative games.

    Hilda Hadan, Leah Zhang-Kennedy, Lennart E. Nacke, and Ville Mäkelä
    2023
    Abs Bib

    Cryptocurrency markets have grown rapidly, attracting millions of casual investors who often lack sufficient financial literacy regarding digital assets, blockchain mechanics, and trading risks. This research report investigates the potential of game-based learning and gamification to improve cryptocurrency education. We conducted a survey of cryptocurrency investors and potential investors to examine their attitudes, concerns, and expectations toward a video game-based learning approach. Our results show that while investors demonstrate moderate trading experience, their self-reported knowledge is limited, highlighting a clear need for educational interventions. We find that participants are highly receptive to gamified education, identifying key game mechanics and features they expect in a learning platform. We discuss design implications for creating effective, engaging, and responsible gamified cryptocurrency education tools.
